What the strategies share

All planner strategies use the same logistics in main.py. They differ only in what they may grow, how many animals and hands they use, and whether they react to market prices.

  • Workers take the nearest job, with watering and feeding first, because a plant or animal is lost after two missed days.
  • Hands are hired each day to match the workload, up to a limit.
  • Land is bought only when the current land is full and cash is available.
  • Nothing is planted that cannot finish before the season ends, and all stock is sold on the last day.

Is this the real engine?

Yes. kaggriculture.py is copied unmodified from Kaggle's kaggle-environments 1.33.3 (Apache 2.0) and runs through Pyodide, which is Python compiled to WebAssembly.

A small runner replaces the parts of Kaggle's framework that cannot run in a browser. A test checks it against the official package, turn by turn.

These are local matches between the strategies in this app. They are not Kaggle leaderboard scores, and strong results here may not hold against other competitors' agents.

How this relates to AgriSense

Kaggriculture is a game with invented prices and crops that grow in days. It shares one idea with the Karnal policy simulator: score a decision rule over a whole season under limited resources. Its results say nothing about real farms or irrigation savings.
